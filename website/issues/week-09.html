Issue 9 of 52  ·  Phase 2 — The Broken Transmission

The Shop Shelf Nobody Stocked — Growing Up Without Male Templates

Imagine walking into a hardware store and finding the shelves empty. Not ransacked — just never stocked. The hooks are there, the labels are there, the layout is organized. But where the tools are supposed to be, there's nothing. Just space.

That's what growing up without male templates looks like. The structure of your upbringing was probably fine. School, family, activities, media. But the specific items that were supposed to be on the shelves — pictures of how to be a man, demonstrated in action, over time, by men you knew personally — those were missing.

Most men your age don't know that's what happened to them. They just know the shelves feel empty.

What a template actually is

A template isn't a role model in the inspirational sense. It's not a poster on the wall or a famous figure you're told to admire. It's something more mundane and more powerful.

A template is a man you watch over time, in ordinary circumstances, making ordinary decisions. You watch him handle a conflict with a neighbor. You watch him deal with a financial setback. You watch him lose something he cared about and keep going. You watch him say no to things that aren't right, even when yes would be easier. You watch him, over and over, being a man — and it builds a picture in you of what that actually looks like.

That picture is what you navigate by. It's the internal model you reference, sometimes without knowing it, when you're facing something new and wondering how to handle it.

Without it, you're navigating blind.

What boys without templates do

A boy without a solid male template doesn't stay blank. He fills the space with whatever's available.

Some fill it with media figures — athletes, actors, influencers, fictional characters. These images are highly filtered, performance-optimized, and stripped of the texture that makes a template useful. You see the highlight reel. You never see the man sitting with his failure at 2am or choosing the harder right thing when nobody is watching.

Some fill it with peers — boys their own age navigating the same deficit. The blind leading the blind, with a lot of confidence and very little orientation.

Some fill it with ideas — internet philosophies, self-help frameworks, content designed to explain manhood to men who never saw it lived. Some of that content is useful. None of it replaces proximity to a real man living it in real time.

The specific things a template provides

When you had a real male template — a father who was present, an uncle, a coach who was genuinely invested — here's what you got, mostly without knowing it:

Evidence that hard things are survivable. When you watched an older man face difficulty and come through it, your nervous system registered: that is possible. Not as an idea, but as a witnessed reality.

A standard of conduct you didn't have to invent. You saw how a man you respected handled anger, disappointment, pressure, and success — and that became your reference point.

Permission to become something. Watching a man live out a full, specific, engaged version of manhood communicates implicitly: this is what you are moving toward, and it is worth moving toward.

Without those three things, a young man is genuinely working from scratch in ways that boys with templates simply are not.

The gap shows up in specific moments

You feel the empty shelf most sharply at specific moments. When you're in a conflict and you don't know how to handle it with strength and without cruelty. When you need to have a hard conversation and you freeze. When you fail at something that matters to you and you have no model for how to process that and keep going. When you're in a leadership position — even a small one — and you feel completely unprepared.

These aren't character flaws. They're gaps. And gaps are fillable.

Filling the shelf deliberately

You can't go back and have the childhood that would have stocked your shelves naturally. But you can do something that works well enough: deliberately seek out men who have what you're missing and spend enough time around them to absorb it.

This is not networking. It's not finding a "mentor" in the corporate sense. It's proximity with attention. Getting close enough to watch how a real man handles the things you haven't figured out yet. Asking questions. Doing the work it takes to be in the right rooms.

The shelf can be stocked. It just requires intention rather than osmosis. That's more work. But it's available to you right now, today, in a way that no previous generation had quite as clearly within reach — because the men who have what you need are findable, accessible, often eager to pass it on.

All that's required is for you to go looking.
