# CTA Options — The Man You Are at 20 vs. The Man You Can Be at 30

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on making the most of the years that still compound. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Years that still compound" creates urgency without being alarmist — it positions the newsletter as relevant to where the reader is now, and implies that this relevance is time-sensitive. Speaks to the young man who just understood why these years matter.

---

## CTA 2 — Decade Audit Prompt

**The line:**
Write this down: In ten years, which version of yourself do you want to be standing on — the one who compounded, or the one who coasted? Then write down the three decisions, made daily, that determine which one shows up. Those three decisions are your operating principles for the next decade.

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Long-horizon planning / personal manifesto initiation

**Tone note:** "Which version do you want to be standing on" is a powerful reframe of the article's central image. The instruction to identify three daily decisions converts the long-horizon thinking into actionable specifics. "Operating principles for the next decade" is ambitious language that the article has earned by making the stakes clear.

---

## CTA 3 — Cohort or Community for Men in Their 20s

**The line:**
If you're in your twenties and you want to be around other men who are taking these years seriously — who are actually building the things that compound — this is where they are.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after establishing what the high-performer at 30 has that the coaster doesn't

**Goal:** Community / cohort membership

**Tone note:** Hyper-targeted to the article's core audience — men in their 20s who've just been told their decade is high-leverage. "Taking these years seriously" is the exact aspiration the article generates. "Where they are" is casual and direct — less of a sales pitch, more of a pointer.
