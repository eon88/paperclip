## Email Body

I want to give you a question that will change how you make decisions.

Not "what do I want?" but "what is required?"

That swap sounds small. It isn't.

Most men use their wants as a compass. The problem is that wants shift — sometimes hourly. You want to get in shape until it means giving up Friday night. You want to build the business until it gets hard. You want the relationship until it asks for something consistent.

The wanting never stops. The doing barely starts.

This is hedonistic drift. It doesn't announce itself. It just stacks — month after month — until you look up and realize you've been chasing things that haven't actually built anything.

**Here's the question that changes the frame.**

"What does this moment require of me?"

Not what do you feel like. What does the situation call for. The relationship, the commitment, the work hour in front of you, the person you said you'd be. That question doesn't care about your mood. It asks what you owe the moment.

You already know the answer almost every time. The wanting question lets you pretend you don't.

**This is also why you have a follow-through problem.**

You started the program because you wanted to. The wanting faded. So did the effort. But "what's required?" is a different kind of agreement with yourself. Not "I'll do this while it feels good." But "I said this mattered — what does it require from me today?"

That's a contract. Contracts don't expire when your mood shifts.

**And here's the part nobody mentions:** when you stop running on want, you actually get more. The man who asks what's required — in his work, his relationships, his character — becomes someone people trust. He earns opportunities. He earns real respect. And quietly, he earns his own.

Which is what most men are actually chasing when they say they want money, freedom, or status. They want to think well of themselves.

You don't get that by asking what you want. You get it by asking what's required — and then doing it.

**Your challenge:** Before Friday, pick one area of your life where you've been defaulting to "what do I want?" — and swap the question. Write down what that commitment actually requires from you this week. Do it before you ask yourself whether you feel like it.

The wanting will still be there. But now it follows something real.

— The Digital Men Letter
