## LinkedIn Post

Most men have a follow-through problem. They start things. They don't finish them. And the reason is simpler than most people admit: they made the commitment based on what they wanted, not what's required.

"What do I want?" sounds like a reasonable question. It isn't — not as a compass. Wants shift hourly. The motivation that got you into the gym three weeks ago has nothing to say about whether you show up today. The excitement that launched the project can't tell you what to do when it gets slow and expensive.

The question that actually moves men forward is different: "What does this moment require of me?"

That question doesn't care about your mood. It asks what you owe the situation — the commitment you made, the relationship in front of you, the version of yourself you said you were building.

Marcus Aurelius wrote to himself: "What am I doing with my soul? Ask yourself this question every hour." That's the same frame. Not am I enjoying this. Not is this what I pictured. Just: what is called for here, and am I giving it?

This is not about eliminating desire. It's about not letting desire run the show.

The man who asks what's required becomes someone people trust. He shows up when it's inconvenient. He delivers when it costs him something. That's the man who earns real respect — including his own.

**Why it works:** Short, sharp, and slightly indicting. It names the self-deception without moralizing. The viewer recognizes himself immediately. High share value — the kind of line someone screenshots and sends to a friend.

**Moment:** "Not 'I'll do this while it feels good.' But 'I said this mattered — what does it require from me today?' Contracts don't expire when your mood shifts."
**Why it works:** Reframes commitment as a contract rather than motivation. Practical and punchy. Lands in under 30 seconds with no context needed. Men who've quit things too early will feel this directly.
