# CTA Options — Stop Asking What You Want

---

## CTA 1 — Reflection Prompt

**The line:** "If this question hit something, write it down. What's required of you this week — one thing, one sentence. Put it somewhere you'll see it."

**Placement:** End of article, immediately after the challenge section.

**Goal:** Drive reflection and personal application. Converts a reader into someone who takes a concrete action tied to the piece.

**Tone note:** Quiet and personal. Not a link, not a product — just a practice. Extends the philosophical reframe into daily life.

---

## CTA 2 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:** "We write every week about what it actually takes to build a life worth having. If this is the kind of thinking you want more of, subscribe."

**Placement:** Bottom of newsletter, after the challenge.

**Goal:** Newsletter subscriber conversion. Positioned for readers who encounter the article through social and want more.

**Tone note:** No pressure, no hype. Matter-of-fact. The content does the selling — the CTA just opens the door.

---

## CTA 3 — Community

**The line:** "Most men need someone to ask them this question out loud. If you're ready to do that work, we're building a community of men who are. Come find us."

**Placement:** End of social posts (LinkedIn and Instagram), or as a pinned comment.

**Goal:** Community or cohort entry point. For men who feel the isolation of trying to grow without other men around them.

**Tone note:** Direct acknowledgment that this work is harder alone. Doesn't oversell — speaks to the felt need the article already surfaces.
