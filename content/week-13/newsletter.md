# Life Compass — Issue 13

**Subject:** What was lost when masculinity became suspect

---

This one requires precision.

Two ways to say it wrong: blame women, or pretend nothing changed. Both are wrong. The honest position is harder — and more useful.

Something did shift in the environments where boys spend most of their time, particularly schools. The classroom moved toward what boys, on average, struggle with: extended sitting, verbal processing, emotional expression as primary currency, the suppression of competition and physical energy. Boys' outcomes began to diverge from girls' in the same period the environment changed. That's not coincidence.

But beyond pedagogy, something subtler happened. A set of qualities once openly cultivated in young men — toughness, directness, physical and moral courage, the willingness to absorb hardship without complaint — gradually moved from celebrated to suspect. Not because those qualities are bad. They aren't. But the institutional response to their misuse was to pathologize the underlying quality rather than teach its right use.

A boy with aggression became a problem to manage, not a boy with energy that needed channeling. A boy who competed needed to be redirected toward collaboration, not given a worthy competition to win. A generation of boys grew up in environments that treated their nature as an inconvenience, with almost no instruction in how to turn that nature into something valuable.

What was lost isn't masculinity itself. What was lost was mature masculinity — the cultivation that turns raw energy and drive into something that can actually serve the people around you.

Raw masculinity without cultivation is destructive. Cultivated masculinity is what the world needs more of. The problem is the cultivation step got dropped out of the process. Not through malice. Through forgetting — or deciding it wasn't worth doing.

Your job isn't to resent what was lost. Your job is to find the cultivation that was skipped and do it yourself.

That process is not just personal development. It's a contribution to something that's been missing for a generation.

**This week's anchor:** Name one quality in yourself — directness, drive, competitiveness — that you've been apologising for. Write down one way to develop it rather than suppress it.

— Life Compass
