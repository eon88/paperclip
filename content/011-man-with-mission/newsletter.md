## Email Body

The men who survived Auschwitz weren't the strongest.

Viktor Frankl saw this firsthand. The survivors were the ones with a reason — a mission, a person, a purpose that made the suffering mean something.

That's not history. That's a survival manual for right now.

The crisis isn't weakness. It's purposelessness.

Depression, passivity, distraction, emptiness — these aren't character flaws. They're what happens when a man doesn't know what he's *for.* A man without a mission doesn't just drift. He drowns. Slowly. In comfort and noise.

And here's the part most men get wrong: they wait for purpose to arrive. They think it's something that happens *to* them — a moment of clarity, a calling from the heavens. It's not.

**Purpose is built. Through action. Through service. Through paying attention to what moves you.**

Three questions to start right now:

1. What problem in the world makes your jaw tighten?
2. What have you survived that someone else is currently going through?
3. What skill or gift do you have that could serve someone?

Those three answers, taken together, are the beginning of your mission. Not the whole map. Just the first move.

A man with a mission doesn't become immune to pain. He still bleeds. He still fails. But he has a reason to get back up. And that reason — when it's real, when it's outward-facing, when it's bigger than you — is the most powerful force a man can carry.

**This week's challenge:** Write down those three answers. One sentence each. Don't overthink it. Just write.

That's where it starts.

— The Digital Men Letter
