## LinkedIn Post

Most men are waiting to find their purpose.

They treat it like something that will arrive one day — a signal, a calling, a moment of clarity. So they wait. And while they wait, they drift.

Viktor Frankl spent years in Nazi concentration camps. He observed that the men who survived weren't the physically strongest. They were the men who had a reason to survive — a mission, a person, a work not yet finished.

This is not abstract philosophy. This is practical survival.

A man without a clear mission is not just directionless. He is vulnerable. To distraction. To depression. To the slow erosion of everything he could become.

Purpose is not found. It is built — at the intersection of the pain that makes you angry, the hardship you've already survived, and the gifts you carry that others need.

If you are a leader, a father, a mentor, a builder — you cannot afford to coast on vague intentions. The people around you need a man who knows what he's for.

Start there. Get clear. Build toward something real.
