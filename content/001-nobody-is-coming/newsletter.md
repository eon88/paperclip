## Email Body

Nobody is coming.

Not the mentor who sees your potential. Not the calling that announces itself. Not the moment of clarity so obvious you can't miss it. You've been waiting for one of those to arrive before you start moving. That's the problem.

You're not lazy. You're not broken. You're doing exactly what twelve years of school trained you to do — wait for the teacher, get the instructions, execute. But you graduated. And the teacher never showed up again. The habit stayed.

So now it's 11pm. Phone in hand. Watching someone else build the thing you told yourself you'd build. Tabs open for the business idea, the certification, the move you've been "thinking about" for eight months. That's not rest. That's the waiting room.

**Here's the distinction that changes things.**

There's a difference between support and rescue. Support walks alongside you while you're already moving. Rescue generates the motion for you. You've been waiting for rescue. But rescue isn't coming. Support can't find you if you're standing still.

Nobody is coming to hand you your purpose. Nobody is coming to make the fear go away before you act. That's your job. It was always your job.

**So how do you start when you have no map?**

You stop waiting for clarity and start trading action for it. Clarity is not what makes movement possible — clarity is what movement produces. The question isn't *what's the right move.* It's *what's the smallest defensible move I can make today.*

Apply for the job. Send the email. Start the project at 60% quality. Publish the thing that isn't ready. You're not looking for a perfect outcome. You're looking for evidence — evidence that you're the kind of man who moves. You won't believe that by thinking about it.

Mentors find men who are already in motion. Opportunity finds the man who made himself visible.

The man on the other side of this doesn't have it figured out. He's just not waiting anymore.

**Your challenge:** Before Friday, take the one action you've been postponing for more than two weeks. Not a plan to take it. The action itself. Then tell one person what you did.

Nobody is coming. But you're still here.

That's enough.

— The Digital Men Letter
