## Email Body

Most men start their day reactively.

Phone first. Email first. Someone else's agenda first. The day begins in response mode — answering, scrolling, catching up — before a single intentional thing has been done. By 9am, the energy has been distributed to other people's priorities and the most important work of the day is already competing with exhaustion.

This is not a productivity problem. It's an ownership problem.

The morning routine is not about waking up at 4am or following an elaborate protocol. It's about one thing: starting the day with something you chose, before the world starts choosing for you.

Here's what that does:

**It sets your identity.** Every morning you keep your commitment — the training session, the reading, the journaling, the prayer — you send a signal to yourself: I am the kind of man who does what he says. That signal compounds. The man who keeps his morning promise for six months is a different man than the one who doesn't, and the difference isn't just in his body or his output. It's in what he believes about himself.

**It protects what matters.** Whatever you put first gets protected. The man who trains first thing gets his training done. The man who reads first thing reads consistently. The man who starts with email gets other people's priorities done consistently.

**It builds the muscle.** The act of doing a hard thing before you do anything easy is a form of practice. It trains the pattern: difficulty first, reward second. That pattern — built in the morning — bleeds into the rest of the day.

**Your challenge this week:** Design your ideal first 60 minutes. Not an elaborate system — three things, in order, every day. Write them down. Do them tomorrow morning before you open your phone. Do it for five days and report back to yourself.

The morning belongs to the man who claims it.
