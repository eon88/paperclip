# CTA Options — Discipline Is Not Punishment — Master Your Morning

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One letter a week. The kind of content you actually want to start your day with. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** The meta-joke works here — an article about owning your morning offering content worth reading in your morning. "The kind of content you actually want to start your day with" is a light touch that ties the CTA directly to the article's subject.

---

## CTA 2 — Morning Design Prompt

**The line:**
Design your ideal first 60 minutes right now. Not an elaborate system. Three things, in order, that matter to who you're becoming. Write them here. Then do them tomorrow before you open your phone. Five days straight.

**Placement:** Closing challenge

**Goal:** Immediate behavioral commitment

**Tone note:** Highly specific and immediately actionable. "Right now" creates urgency. "Not an elaborate system" pre-empts the over-engineering tendency. The five-day framing makes it feel achievable rather than permanent. "Before you open your phone" is the concrete rule that makes it executable.

---

## CTA 3 — 30-Day Morning Challenge Awareness

**The line:**
If you want the full 30-day morning protocol — what to do, in what order, and why — we built it. It's designed to be done in 60 minutes or less and it changes everything.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after explaining what a consistent morning practice actually produces

**Goal:** Product or lead magnet awareness (30-day morning challenge)

**Tone note:** "We built it" is direct and confident. "60 minutes or less" addresses the most common objection (I don't have time). "Changes everything" is strong but lands after the article has already established why the morning matters — so it doesn't feel like hyperbole, it feels like the natural conclusion.
