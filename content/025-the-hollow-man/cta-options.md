# CTA Options — The Hollow Man: What Happens When You Live Only for Yourself

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on building a life worth living — not just a life that looks good from the outside. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Worth living vs. looks good" directly mirrors the article's distinction between meaningful and hollow. The reader who connected with the article will feel this framing immediately.

---

## CTA 2 — Contribution Audit

**The line:**
Before you do anything else today: answer this honestly. What am I building right now, and who does it serve beyond me? Write one sentence. Read it back. Does it feel true? Does it feel worth it?

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Self-directed reflection / prompt

**Tone note:** Short, demanding, and personal. "Does it feel worth it?" is the final needle — not guilt, but an honest question that the man who has read the article is now equipped to answer more honestly than before. The "feel true" and "feel worth it" are separate — something can feel true (accurately described) and still not worth it (motivationally insufficient).

---

## CTA 3 — Mission Clarity Workshop

**The line:**
The outward orientation the article describes starts with knowing what you're for. If that's unclear — if the honest answer is "mostly me and my comfort" and you want to change it — that's where we start.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after establishing that meaning is found by pointing outward

**Goal:** Workshop, course, or coaching awareness (mission/purpose clarity)

**Tone note:** "That's where we start" is direct and humble — not "we'll fix you" but "we begin here." The acknowledgment that the honest answer might be uncomfortable ("mostly me and my comfort") mirrors the article's honesty and builds trust with the reader who just admitted that to himself.
