## Email Body

There's a version of success that looks right from the outside and feels empty on the inside.

The man who hit the goals. Made the money. Got the freedom he wanted. And somewhere around his 30s starts to notice that the life he built for himself, by himself, feels like an empty room. Clean. Quiet. And hollow.

This isn't a mystery. It's predictable.

**Meaning is not stored in achievement.** It's not stored in comfort, status, or the accumulation of things you wanted. Viktor Frankl — who watched men die in concentration camps and observed who maintained dignity and who didn't — concluded that meaning is always found by pointing outward, not inward. Toward a cause, a person, a contribution.

The man who turns entirely inward in search of meaning finds a mirror, not a source.

**Selfishness is a slow form of suicide.** Each year the circle of concern stays fixed at "me, mine, my comfort" — the muscle that generates meaning atrophies. It gets harder to care. Harder to feel real engagement. Harder to find a reason to get up.

The men who age well are the ones who gave something real — jobs created, children raised with care, community served, work that outlasted them. The emptiness ends when you turn outward.

**This week:** Ask yourself honestly — what am I building, and who benefits from it beyond me? If the honest answer is "mostly just me," that's not condemnation. That's information. Use it to redirect even slightly.

One degree of outward orientation changes the whole trajectory over time.
