# CTA Options — You Will Be Tested. Are You Ready?

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One letter a week. Straight talk on what it takes to be ready for a life that doesn't go easy on you. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "A life that doesn't go easy on you" echoes the article's core premise — that difficulty is inevitable and preparation is the only real response. Speaks directly to the reader who just accepted that premise.

---

## CTA 2 — Personal Audit Prompt

**The line:**
Today's honest question: if your hardest moment arrived this week — physical, financial, relational, or spiritual — how ready are you? Rate yourself 1–10 in each category. Then decide what the number tells you to do next.

**Placement:** Closing challenge / end of article

**Goal:** Self-assessment / behavioral activation

**Tone note:** Concrete and uncomfortable in a useful way. The article argues for preparation — this prompt forces the reader to measure his actual current readiness. The "decide what the number tells you" framing gives the reader agency without prescribing the answer.

---

## CTA 3 — Program or Course Awareness

**The line:**
If you want a structured approach to building the physical, mental, and character foundations — before you need them — that's what this is designed to do.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after the "the test is coming, you just don't know when" moment

**Goal:** Program, course, or cohort sign-up

**Tone note:** Lands at the moment the reader has accepted the premise (tests are coming, preparation is now) but before the article has given him the full DIY framework. Creates desire for a structured solution without being pushy. "Before you need them" is the key phrase — it mirrors the article's urgency without manufacturing fear.
