# Life Compass — Issue 42

**Subject:** Why results aren't showing up yet

---

You've done the work. Made the commitments. Shown up when you didn't feel like it. And the results? Still quiet. Still invisible. Still not there.

This is the part no one talks about honestly. The months in the middle — when you can't tell the difference between persistence and delusion.

What you're in is called the lag. Every worthwhile thing has one. There's always a gap between when you start doing the right things and when those things produce visible results. Seeds don't show you anything for weeks. The ground looks exactly the same after you plant as it did before. But something irreversible is already happening beneath the surface.

Your brain is wired for immediate feedback. When the feedback doesn't come, doubt fills the silence. You start reading the absence of results as evidence that nothing is working. But absence of visible results is not the same as nothing happening.

When you're three months into building something, don't measure outcomes. Measure process. Are you showing up? Are you getting better — even slowly? Are you solving problems you couldn't have solved six months ago? That's the data that matters right now. Measuring outcomes before they can exist is like digging up a seed to check if it's growing.

Three temptations show up when nothing seems to be working. The urge to pivot — often disguised as wisdom, really just an escape from discomfort. Ask yourself: am I changing direction because I have new information, or because I'm uncomfortable? Then comparison — which gives you false data and steals your attention. What you see of someone else's progress leaves out their starting point, their resources, their luck. And acceleration — trying to compress the timeline by doing more, faster. You can't outrun a lag. You can only exhaust yourself.

What actually carries you through isn't motivation. Motivation is weather — it comes and goes. What carries you through is clarity and a decision made in advance. Not "I'll keep going if it seems to be working" but "I'm going until I have clear evidence this specific direction is wrong." The decision is already made. You just execute it each day.

There's also something quieter that matters: becoming a man who does hard things without applause. The lag is a teacher. It shows you whether you're working for the result or for the work itself.

**This week's anchor:** Track your process, not your outcomes — write down the three most important actions you took this week and evaluate how well you did them, not whether they produced results yet.

— Life Compass
