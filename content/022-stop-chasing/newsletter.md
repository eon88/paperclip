**Preview text:** The man who is always pursuing is communicating one thing: they are above me. Stop communicating that. Here's what to do instead.

---

## Email Body

Here's the trap most young men fall into:

They identify something they want — a woman, a status, approval, a following — and they put their energy into pursuit. Texts, performances, compromises, strategies. All the energy pointing outward, toward the thing that keeps moving away.

And the harder they push, the further it moves.

This is not bad luck. It is the mechanism. When you chase something, you communicate your position: you need them, they don't need you. Every act of visible pursuit reinforces that signal. And people — women, employers, potential friends, anyone — move toward confidence and away from need.

**The fix is not strategy. It's direction.**

Stop asking "how do I get what I want?" Start asking "what kind of man attracts what I want?"

Then become that man — not as a technique, but because the becoming is worth doing for its own reasons.

The man who knows what he's building and is building it right now carries a different energy. He has direction. He has real competence. He holds standards. He doesn't need to perform because he doesn't need the approval.

That man doesn't chase. He builds. And what he's building attracts.

**This week:** Identify one thing you've been chasing that isn't returning your investment. Not ruthlessly — honestly. Then redirect that energy toward something you're building. One conversation, one session, one hour of real work on something that matters.

The build always beats the chase.

— The Digital Men Letter
