# CTA Options — Books Every Young Man Should Read: Series Launch

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up for Series

**The line:**
Subscribe to receive each book deep-dive as we release them — the core argument, the key passages, and the three things to actually do with it.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up tied to the ongoing book series

**Tone note:** "The three things to actually do with it" is the differentiator — this is not summaries for passive consumption, but active application prompts. For readers who just been told that passive reading is entertainment, this framing confirms that the series will be useful rather than just interesting.

---

## CTA 2 — Reading Commitment

**The line:**
Pick the book on the list that most directly speaks to what you're wrestling with right now. Order it or download it today. Read one chapter before your phone tomorrow morning. That's the whole commitment. Make it.

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Immediate book purchase / reading initiation

**Tone note:** "Most directly speaks to what you're wrestling with" is a personalizing instruction — it moves the reader from "which one should I start with" to "what do I actually need right now?" The rest reduces the friction to minimum: one chapter, before the phone, tomorrow. Executable in less than 30 minutes.

---

## CTA 3 — Book Club or Discussion Community

**The line:**
Reading alone is half the value. If you want to read these with other men — to discuss what landed, argue about what you disagree with, and hold each other to actually applying what you read — the community is where that happens.

**Placement:** After explaining how to read actively (annotations, stopping at hard passages)

**Goal:** Community membership / book club cohort

**Tone note:** "Argue about what you disagree with" is the key phrase — it positions the community discussion as intellectual engagement, not passive agreement. This is appealing to men who want to think, not just absorb. "Hold each other to actually applying" ties the reading to behavioral change, which is the whole point of the active reading framework.
