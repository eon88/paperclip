# Men Worth Learning From: Why We're Starting This Series

Every man needs older men in his life. Men who've already walked through the fires he's approaching. Men who've built something, lost something, recovered, rebuilt. Men who tell the truth about what it actually took.

Most young men don't have them. Not in their families, not in their communities, not on their social media feeds. What they have instead is content — motivational clips, highlight reels, hustle porn, theory. Useful sometimes. But not the same as sitting across from a man who has lived it and asking: what do you actually know?

This series is an attempt to fix that.

---

## Why Conversation Is Different

Books give you concepts. Podcasts give you frameworks. Articles give you principles. Conversations give you something else — the texture of a real life, the specific turning points, the things that actually worked versus the things that sounded right but failed, the regrets a man carries and what he's learned from them.

When you hear a man describe the year his business almost collapsed, and how he held it together, and what he wishes he'd done differently — that information travels differently than anything you can read. It's not abstract. It's not optimized for shareability. It's real. And real things stick.

There's also something you can learn from watching how a man carries himself that you can't get from his words. Does he speak with certainty or with humility? Does he own his failures or explain them away? Does he give real answers or polished ones? The conversation makes those things visible in a way that a polished essay cannot.

The best education most young men will ever get is conversation with men who have done things. This series is an attempt to bring that to more people.

---

## Who We're Looking For

Not celebrities. Not men with the biggest following or the loudest brand. Men who have built something real — a business, a family, a community, a body of work — and have the kind of depth that comes from having paid the full price for what they know.

Men who have failed seriously and recovered. Men who have raised children and are honest about what it took. Men who have built businesses and can tell the truth about what nobody puts in the case study. Men who have wrestled with real questions about faith, meaning, discipline, and purpose — and come to actual conclusions, not performance answers.

Men who are direct. Who don't perform wisdom but actually possess it. Who have earned the right to speak by doing the work.

We're not interested in the polished version. We're interested in the true version. The conversation that would be useful to a 22-year-old trying to figure out what to do with his life.

---

## What the Conversations Will Cover

Each interview will go wherever it needs to go. But there are certain questions that will appear in every one, because they matter for every man:

**On the mission:** What are you building and why? How did you find the thing worth building? What did you have to walk away from to pursue it?

**On difficulty:** What was the hardest period of your life? What did you learn from it that you couldn't have learned any other way? What do you know now about hardship that you wish someone had told you earlier?

**On relationships:** How did the people around you — for better or worse — shape who you became? What does a good brotherhood look like to you? What have you gotten right and wrong in your closest relationships?

**On character:** What standard do you hold yourself to that most people around you don't? Where did that standard come from? What does it cost you?

**On regret:** What would you do differently? Not as self-flagellation — as real information for men who still have the time to make different choices.

These are not soft questions. We're not looking for inspirational answers. We're looking for true ones.

---

## Why This Matters Now

The generation of men coming up right now is growing up with less direct male mentorship than possibly any generation before them. Fathers absent or disconnected. Communities fragmented. The men who would have served as models — coaches, uncles, older friends — are less present than they used to be.

Into that gap has come an enormous amount of content about masculinity — some useful, most of it either too soft or too performative to actually help. Young men are hungry for something real. For men who will tell them the truth, not what sounds good.

This series is a small attempt to give that. Real men. Real conversations. No performance.

If you're reading this as a young man trying to figure out what you're supposed to do with your life — pay attention. Not because these men have all the answers. But because the right question asked to the right man at the right moment can change the direction of a life. That's happened to me. I want it to happen to more men.
