# CTA Options — Dopamine Is Destroying Your Ambition

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
If this landed, you need the letter. One piece a week. No noise, no hacks — just the straight truth about building a serious life.

**Placement:** End of article, below the Friday challenge

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** The article ends with a direct command. This CTA steps back and makes a low-friction, high-credibility offer. "No noise, no hacks" positions the newsletter as the antithesis of everything the article just argued against — cheap stimulation dressed as value. The reader has just been asked to subtract; this gives him one thing worth adding.

## CTA 2 — Reflection Prompt

**The line:**
Before you open a single app — write down the three biggest sources of cheap stimulation in your life right now. You already know what they are.

**Placement:** End of article, above any links or offers

**Goal:** Reflection prompt / behavioral activation

**Tone note:** The article spends its entire length explaining the mechanism. This prompt converts that understanding into immediate self-diagnosis. No link. No ask. The phrase "you already know" mirrors the article's argument that the problem isn't knowledge — it's action. Leaves the reader indicted in the best way.

---

## CTA 3 — Lead Magnet / Product Awareness

**The line:**
Knowing your dopamine system is wrecked is step one. The harder problem is building a daily structure that protects it — and makes the work feel inevitable. That's what this is about.

**Placement:** Mid-article — after the "What Overstimulation Actually Does to You" section, before the reset protocol

**Goal:** Product or lead magnet awareness (e.g., a 30-day focus reset guide or daily structure framework)

**Tone note:** The article diagnoses the problem in depth before offering the solution. This CTA lands at the moment the reader understands the stakes but hasn't yet received the fix. It doesn't promise to do the work for him — it promises structure that makes doing the work easier. "Inevitable" is precise: it implies systems, not heroics, which is exactly what a man with a trashed dopamine system needs to hear.
