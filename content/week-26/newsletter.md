# Life Compass — Issue 26

**Subject:** The values you live by — did you choose them?

---

Most men operate on borrowed values.

Principles absorbed from their environment without ever being consciously examined or chosen. They work hard because their father did. They distrust authority because of something in high school. They pursue money because that's what success looked like where they grew up.

These values are real. But they are not your code. They are a first draft written by other people and other circumstances.

A man who has never examined that draft can be moved off his foundation by pressure, temptation, or a compelling argument at the wrong moment. The work of writing your own code is the work of becoming ungovernable by accident.

A personal code is not a list of aspirations. Aspirations bend under pressure. A code is load-bearing — the principles you've decided you will hold regardless of circumstances, because you've decided they define who you are. It governs how you treat people, what you owe them, what you will and will not do regardless of what they're doing to you.

Real values don't come from reflection alone. They come from experience. You discover them in the moments of deepest discomfort — when you compromised something and felt it afterward. When someone treated you in a way that felt fundamentally wrong. When you let someone down and couldn't shake the weight of it. These moments are pointing at something you care about more than you knew.

Write it down. Not qualities — commitments. Not "I am honest" but "I will say what I actually think, including when it's uncomfortable." Not "I am disciplined" but "I do what I said I would do, especially when I don't feel like it." The specificity matters. The more precisely you can state the principle, the harder it is to rationalize your way around it.

Then test it: look at the decisions you've made in the past year. Does your code explain the ones you're proud of? Does its absence explain the ones you're not?

Here is the real question your code has to answer: what will you not do?

The man who knows what he will not do has the most important kind of freedom — the freedom from being available to his worst moments. He has already made the hard decisions before the pressure arrived.

Write the line. Hold it.

**This week's anchor:** Write three commitments — not qualities you aspire to, but lines you will not cross. Be specific enough that they could actually govern a real decision.

— Life Compass
