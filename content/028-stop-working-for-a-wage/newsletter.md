## Email Body

Most men are running a strategy that guarantees they'll always need to keep working.

Trade time for money. Spend money. Repeat until retirement. Never accumulate enough of anything that generates value when you're not actively selling your time.

This is not a moral failure. It's a design failure. And most men were never given the alternative design.

**The distinction:** A wage is income — money exchanged for hours. Wealth is accumulated assets that generate return without requiring your time. The man who only has income is one bad month away from real trouble. The man who has assets has options.

**The shift isn't about getting rich quick.** It's about changing the question from "how do I earn more?" to "how do I own more?" The employee mindset asks: what can I get paid to do? The builder mindset asks: what can I create, acquire, or build that has value beyond my time?

This shift starts small:
- Savings rate before spending, not after
- Skills that are transferable and marketable, not just performed for one employer
- A project, side business, or investment — however small — that you own

**The point is the orientation, not the scale.** A man who earns $40,000 a year and saves 20%, builds a side skill, and owns one thing — a small business, a rental, a digital product — is operating on a fundamentally different trajectory than a man who earns $100,000 and spends $100,000.

This week: ask yourself where on the spectrum you actually are. Not where you want to be — where you are. Then identify one thing you could start owning that's currently just costing you time.
