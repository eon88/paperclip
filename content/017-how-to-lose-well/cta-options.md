# CTA Options — Failure Is Not the End. Giving Up Is.

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on what it takes to keep building through the hard parts. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Keep building through the hard parts" is on-brand with the article and positions the newsletter as the ongoing resource for exactly this kind of ongoing difficulty. Short and unpretentious.

---

## CTA 2 — Debrief Worksheet / Reflection Prompt

**The line:**
Use this now: think of your biggest failure from the last 12 months. Write one honest answer to each of these — What happened? What did I contribute? What would I do differently? What am I taking forward? 20 minutes. No self-pity, no excuses.

**Placement:** End of article, as the closing challenge

**Goal:** Immediate behavioral action / self-directed debrief exercise

**Tone note:** Converts the article's framework into a concrete exercise. The "no self-pity, no excuses" framing maintains the article's voice — it's not therapy, it's analysis. The time limit (20 minutes) makes it feel achievable rather than overwhelming. No link needed — just a prompt that does real work.

---

## CTA 3 — Coaching or Mentorship Awareness

**The line:**
The debrief is hardest to run alone — it's easy to slip into self-blame or self-defense without someone who's been through it to hold the mirror. That's what good mentorship does. If you're looking for that, it exists here.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after explaining the debrief process

**Goal:** Coaching, mentorship program, or community awareness

**Tone note:** Acknowledges a real limitation of the self-directed debrief (the difficulty of honest self-assessment without an outside perspective). Positions mentorship/coaching as a solution to that specific limitation — not as a general "level up" pitch. Honest about what it's for.
