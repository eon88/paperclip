# CTA Options — The Brotherhood Gap

> **Format:** CTA Options | **Idea:** 007 | **Status:** Ready to publish

---

## CTA 1 — Community / Direct Invitation

**The line:**
If you read this and felt it — that's not an accident. The first step isn't a framework. It's a conversation with one real man. We're building a space where that's normal. If you want in, come find us.

**Placement:** End of article, immediately after the challenge section.

**Goal:** Drive warm traffic to a community, program, or private group — wherever The Digital Men Company hosts its core audience.

**Tone note:** Heavy and personal. The article ends with a challenge to reach out to one man. This CTA extends that invitation — the reader doesn't have to build from scratch alone. Doesn't promise a transformation or oversell. Just opens the door. "Come find us" is an invitation from a group that's already moving, not a pitch from a brand.

---

## CTA 2 — Honest Reflection Prompt

**The line:**
Before you close this — sit with one question. Not the 2am list. This one: when did you last let another man actually know what's going on with you? Not a complaint. Not surface talk. The real thing. If you can't remember, that's your answer.

**Placement:** End of article or end of newsletter, above any links. Works as a standalone standalone pull-quote on social.

**Goal:** Reflection prompt — no link, no product. Designed to stop the reader before he moves on, hold the discomfort the article created, and let it do the real work.

**Tone note:** The heaviest of the three options. No ask. No offer. Just a question that lands where the article left off. This is the CTA for a man who isn't ready to act yet but needs to feel the weight a little longer. The goal is conviction, not conversion. Conviction leads to action — and eventually, to community.

---

## CTA 3 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
We write one piece like this every week. No noise. No performance. Just honest thinking for men who are trying to build something real — starting with themselves. If that's you, get on the list.

**Placement:** Mid-article — best placed after the "What Isolation Actually Does to a Man" section, before the reader has reached the challenge. Or as a P.S. line at the bottom of the newsletter email.

**Goal:** Email list growth.

**Tone note:** Low pressure, clear positioning. "No noise. No performance" echoes the article's critique of men who perform connection without building it. "Starting with themselves" positions the newsletter as a personal formation tool, not a content product. Respects the reader's time and assumes he's capable of deciding for himself — which is exactly the energy the article builds toward.
