## Twitter/X Thread

**1/**
There used to be a specific moment when a boy became a man.

Witnessed. Marked. Celebrated.

That moment no longer exists.

And that absence is destroying a generation.

---

**2/**
It wasn't symbolic. It was a structured rupture.

The boy faced something genuinely hard. Older men held the standard. The community witnessed it.

He didn't age into manhood. He was pulled through a threshold.

Every culture that produced capable men had some version of this.

---

**3/**
Then it was quietly dismantled.

What replaced it?

Nothing.

18th birthday. Graduation. A diploma and a job market with no map.

The result: a long, undefined adolescence with no visible end.

---

**4/**
Even present, engaged fathers couldn't fix this.

Because they didn't have it either.

This isn't about broken homes. It's about a cultural technology that no single man can rebuild alone.

You're holding the end of a severed line.

---

**5/**
Real initiation has three elements.

A genuine test — hard enough that you don't know if you'll pass.
A mentor who tells you the truth and holds the standard.
A public declaration your community witnesses.

You can construct these. It takes deliberate work. But it's available.

---

**6/**
The standard still exists.

A man can be depended on. He walks toward hard things. He builds rather than consumes. He tells the truth when it costs him.

You were supposed to be handed that standard formally.

You weren't. That's true.

It's not an excuse.

---

**7/**
Your challenge this week:

Find one real man in your life who is further down the path than you.

Ask him: "What do you wish someone had told you at my age?"

Then just listen.

That's the first move.

## LinkedIn Post

Most men in their twenties are living in an in-between that has no name.

Not boys. Not men. Technically adults, but never formally initiated into it.

They're not lazy or weak. They were never given what their grandfathers received: a clear passage into manhood, witnessed by older men, with a standard attached.

Across cultures and centuries, every society that produced capable, grounded men had some form of initiation. Not a party — a structured rupture. A hard test, an elder who held the line, and a community that witnessed the crossing.

That structure is gone. And nobody replaced it.

The result is predictable. Men drift. They avoid commitment because they've never been accountable to anything hard enough to build real identity. They feel like boys in a man's world because, structurally, nothing ever confirmed otherwise.

This isn't a soft problem. It has hard consequences — in relationships, in work, in the culture broadly.

The fix isn't to feel bad about the gap. It's to start building what was missing.

Find a real test. Find a mentor who tells you the truth. Declare something publicly and let people hold you to it.

The threshold is still there. It's waiting for you to choose it.
