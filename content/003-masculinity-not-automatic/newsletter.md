## Email Body

There used to be a moment — a specific moment — when a boy became a man.

Not a birthday. Not graduation. A real passage. Something hard, witnessed by older men, marking a clear line between what you were and what you were now required to become.

That moment no longer exists.

And in its place? A long, undefined drift. Men in their twenties with no clear threshold behind them and no clear standard in front of them. Expected to adult, but never formally initiated into it.

---

This isn't about broken homes or absent fathers. Even men who had present, engaged dads mostly didn't get what initiation requires — because their fathers didn't have it either. The chain broke a generation or two back. Most men are holding the end of a severed line.

The loss is real. Real initiation had three things working at once: a genuine test with stakes, an elder who held the standard, and a public declaration that the community witnessed. Strip all three and you get what we have now — men who are technically adults but have never been pulled through a threshold.

---

You can't wait for culture to rebuild what it dismantled. But you can construct the bones yourself.

Find a test hard enough that you don't know in advance if you'll pass. Find a mentor — a real man in your actual life, not a podcast host — who tells you the truth. Declare something, out loud, to people who will hold you to it.

That's not a substitute for initiation. But it's the direction.

---

The standard still exists. A man can be depended on. He walks toward hard things. He builds rather than consumes. He tells the truth when it costs him.

You were supposed to be handed that standard formally, by men who had received it themselves. You weren't. That's worth acknowledging honestly.

But it is not an excuse.

---

**This week's challenge:**

Find one man in your actual life who is further along the path than you. Not someone you follow online — someone you can call.

Ask him one question: *What do you wish someone had told you at my age?*

Then listen. Don't explain yourself. Just take it in.

That conversation is the first step toward finding the men who can hold the standard for you — and eventually, toward becoming the man who holds it for someone else.

The threshold is still there. Walk through it.
