## Email Body

There's a version of you that only exists on the other side of difficulty.

Not on the other side of more planning. Not after another book, another course, another "getting ready to get ready." On the other side of the hard thing you're currently circling.

This week's piece is about what actually happens to men who avoid difficulty long enough.

It's not dramatic. That's the trap. The comfortable man doesn't collapse — he just quietly becomes smaller. His tolerance for discomfort narrows. His self-trust erodes. And one day he finds himself standing in front of something he wants — a bigger role, a harder challenge, a life that actually fits him — and he realizes he doesn't believe he can handle it.

Not because he failed. Because he stopped practicing.

The men worth knowing have a story. Usually a hard one. A period where things fell apart and they had to figure out, on their own, how to put them back together. They're not nostalgic for the pain. But they know what it gave them — and they know it couldn't have come from anywhere else.

Real competence comes from having done hard things. There is no shortcut around that.

**This week's challenge:** You already know what you're avoiding. It came to mind before I finished that sentence. Go take one real step toward it today — not a big step, just a real one.

That's how it starts. One decision to stop looking away.
