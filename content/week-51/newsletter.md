# Life Compass — Issue 51

**Subject:** The year-end review most men get wrong

---

Most people review their year by opening a notes app, scanning vague memories, and feeling either okay because nothing terrible happened or disappointed because nothing spectacular did. Then they make resolutions, forget them by February, and repeat the cycle.

That's a ritual that produces the feeling of reflection without the substance of it.

A real year-end review is uncomfortable. It requires you to look at what you actually did — not what you planned or hoped or intended. Done well, it produces not just a record of the past year but a map for the next one.

Don't measure whether you hit your goals. Goals are useful guides but they are not the measure of a good year. A man who hit $140k while building an excellent foundation may have had a far better year than the man who hit $200k while burning relationships and running on empty.

Measure these instead.

Character and integrity: Were you the man you said you wanted to be, in the ordinary moments — not the highlights? Did you keep your word? Did you face hard things or defer them indefinitely?

Work and mission: Did the work move you toward what you're building? Are you clearer about your direction than a year ago, or hazier?

Relationships: Did the important people in your life get your actual attention — not just your time? Did you show up for anyone who needed it?

Body and mind: Did you treat your physical and mental health as consistent priorities, not emergency projects?

Contribution: Did you give anything? Time, skill, presence. Did you reach back and help someone who was where you were a year ago?

How to do this well: set aside two to three hours. Go month by month, not year at a glance — reconstruct the actual texture of each month using your calendar, journal, photos. Write it down; the act of writing forces clarity that thinking alone doesn't produce. Then make one or two real changes for next year — not fifteen resolutions, one or two. The man who changes two things fully produces more than the man who intends fifteen and changes none.

**This week's anchor:** Do the full review — two to three hours, month by month, written down. Name what you're proud of, what you regret, and one or two things you're actually changing next year.

— Life Compass
