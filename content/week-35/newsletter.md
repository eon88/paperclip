# Life Compass — Issue 35

**Subject:** You don't have a time problem. You have an energy leak.

---

You've tried to fix your schedule. You've blocked your calendar, found the system, arranged the tasks. And you're still exhausted and stuck.

Because time isn't the problem. Energy is.

A man at 40% capacity for twelve hours produces less than a man at 90% for six. The constraint isn't hours — it's the state you're in when you show up to those hours. If you're chronically tired, low-motivated, unable to sustain effort on the things that matter, the question isn't "where do I find more time?" It's "where is my energy going?"

Here are the leaks most men don't see.

Unresolved decisions. Every decision you're sitting on but haven't made runs in the background like an open application draining the battery. The conversation you need to have. The direction you're still deferring. The commitment you said you'd make. These don't wait quietly — they cost you energy every day you don't resolve them. Making the decision stops the drain. Deferral sustains it indefinitely.

Unresolved conflict. Tension with a person in your life — a friend, partner, colleague — is an enormous ongoing drain. You replay conversations. You manage low-grade stress. You think about it when you're trying to do something else. The resolution, even if it's uncomfortable, costs far less than the indefinite tax of avoidance.

Your phone. Put this plainly: the way most men use their smartphones destroys concentration. Not because using a phone is wrong — but because picking it up every few minutes throughout your work period creates constant partial attention, and partial attention is the enemy of everything that actually matters. You cannot do meaningful work while checking in on a feed every ninety seconds. You're not taking breaks. You're dismantling your ability to focus and then wondering why nothing gets done.

Put the phone in another room for two to three hour blocks. Do the actual work. This is not complicated.

Unreal yeses. Every commitment you took on without actually wanting to do it costs energy twice — once to do it, once to manage the resentment of having your time taken by something that didn't deserve it. Every yes has a cost. Saying no cleanly and early is an energy management practice, not a character flaw.

And the thing most men miss: energy requires real recovery. Not scrolling. Not half-working. Actual rest. A walk outside without headphones. Twenty minutes off. A conversation that doesn't demand anything from you. The man who works hard and recovers deliberately beats the man who works and half-rests forever.

This week, run an energy audit. At the end of each day, note what depleted you and what restored you. Three days of honest tracking will show you the patterns. Minimize what drains without return. Protect what energizes.

Energy is the resource. Start managing it directly.

**This week's anchor:** For three days, track what depletes your energy and what restores it — then cut one major leak.

— Life Compass
