# CTA Options — Why "Follow Your Passion" Is Terrible Advice

---

## CTA 1 — Lead Magnet / Framework

**The line:** "Download the Skill Commitment Framework — a one-page tool to help you pick the right skill and build a 90-day plan around it."

**Placement:** End of article, after the Challenge section.

**Goal:** Lead generation. Turns a reader into a subscriber with something concrete and immediately useful.

**Tone note:** Practical and specific. "Framework" signals structure. "90-day" signals commitment without overwhelm. Do not over-promise — promise a tool.

---

## CTA 2 — 7-Day Challenge

**The line:** "Join the 7-Day Work Commitment Challenge — one skill, one action per day, no motivation required."

**Placement:** End of Twitter thread, Instagram caption, or as a pinned story/link.

**Goal:** Community activation and habit formation. Gets men moving immediately.

**Tone note:** "No motivation required" addresses the exact objection passive men have. Challenge framing respects their competitiveness.

---

## CTA 3 — Direction Tool

**The line:** "Not sure which skill to build? Answer 5 questions and we'll give you a clear starting point."

**Placement:** Mid-article after the "How to Start" section. Works as a pop-up or inline card.

**Goal:** Reduce friction for the paralyzed reader. A short quiz meets them exactly where they are.

**Tone note:** The question format mirrors the reader's psychological state — uncertain, looking for direction. Keep it direct: answer questions, get an answer.
