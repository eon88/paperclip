# Social Posts — Why "Follow Your Passion" Is Terrible Advice

---

## Twitter/X Thread

**1/**
"Follow your passion" is the most paralyzing advice ever given to young men.

Here's why — and what to do instead. 🧵

**2/**
Passion is not a starting point. It's a result.

You don't find it first. You build it by getting good at something.

The feeling comes after the work — not before.

**3/**
Most men are stuck because they're waiting for internal clarity before taking external action.

But the clarity comes FROM the action. That's the sequence.

It's backwards for most of us.

**4/**
Think about the men you respect most.

Did they find their passion first? Or did they show up, work hard for years, and eventually become someone who couldn't imagine doing anything else?

**5/**
Skill builds identity.

When you get genuinely good at something, it changes who you are. Competence rewires your self-concept.

That's what people mistake for passion.

**6/**
You don't need a calling. You need a direction.

Pick something hard, learnable, and available right now. Start. You adjust as you go.

**7/**
Every year you spend waiting for passion is a year of compounding skill lost.

Sparks don't build fires. You do — with fuel, friction, and time.

Stop waiting to feel it. Start building it.

## LinkedIn Post

Most young men aren't stuck because they lack ambition. They're stuck because they're asking the wrong question.

They ask: "What am I passionate about?"

They should ask: "What skill can I build that the world will pay me for?"

Research — and reality — says the same thing: passion follows mastery, not the other way around. Cal Newport calls it the craftsman mindset. Stop asking what the world can offer you. Start asking what you can offer the world. Build rare skills. Get genuinely good at something hard. The meaning and motivation grow from the competence — not before it.

The men who love what they do didn't start that way. They built their way there.

If you're leading young men — in your organization, your family, your community — this is worth sharing. The passion-first model is keeping them stuck.

The question isn't whether you're ready. It's: what skill could you start building today?
