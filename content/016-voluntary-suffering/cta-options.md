## CTA 2 — 30-Day Challenge Prompt

**The line:**
Your challenge: pick one form of voluntary difficulty and do it for 30 days without missing a day. Cold shower, daily training, morning fast, phone off until noon. One thing. 30 days. Track what it does to you.

**Placement:** Closing challenge section

**Goal:** Behavioral commitment / 30-day challenge initiation

**Tone note:** The article's argument is that voluntary difficulty builds capacity. This CTA converts that argument into a concrete, time-bound commitment. "Track what it does to you" positions the reader as a scientist studying himself — which is accurate and removes the moralistic framing. The specificity of options makes it easy to start.

## CTA 3 — Spartan Reset Program Awareness

**The line:**
If you want the full 30-day protocol — the exact daily structure that builds every dimension of capacity — that's what the Spartan Reset is designed to do.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after explaining what voluntary hardship looks like in practice

**Goal:** Program/product awareness (30-day challenge product or the content piece 032)

**Tone note:** The reader has just understood why voluntary suffering matters and is being given the concept of a structured protocol. This CTA introduces the existence of a specific, structured version. "Exact daily structure" appeals to the man who is convinced but doesn't know how to translate the concept into action. Short and direct — doesn't over-explain.
