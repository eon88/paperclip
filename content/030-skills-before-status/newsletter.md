## Email Body

There's a specific type of broke young man.

He's got the right clothes. Maybe the right car on a payment he can't really afford. The right social media presence. He knows which restaurants to post from, what watches matter, what brand of everything to wear. He's good at status.

And he is utterly unprepared for any situation that requires him to actually produce something.

**Status and skills are both forms of social capital. But they work entirely differently.**

Status depreciates. The car ages. The outfit goes out of style. The followers unfollow. The social position requires constant maintenance and constant spending to hold. It produces nothing on its own.

Skills compound. The man who spends five years getting genuinely good at something — writing, coding, sales, building, leading, designing — exits year five with a capability that generates income, opportunity, and genuine confidence that can't be faked or bought.

The young man who chose status over skills at 22 is still spending money to maintain the appearance of a person worth knowing. The young man who chose skills over status at 22 has something real — and the appearance follows naturally from the reality.

**The sequence:** Skills → Substance → Real status (which comes without being chased)

This week: identify the most valuable skill you could build in the next 12 months. Not the most impressive-sounding one — the most practically valuable one for what you're trying to build. Spend 30 minutes a day on it for one month. See what compounds.

— The Digital Men Letter
