# CTA Options — Skills Before Status

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on building real capability, not just impressive appearances. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Real capability, not just impressive appearances" is the exact distinction the article makes. Direct callback that confirms the newsletter's position alongside the article's argument.

---

## CTA 2 — Skill-Building Commitment

**The line:**
This week: write down the one skill that would most change your options in the next two years if you built it seriously. Then write down what 30 minutes a day for 60 days on that skill would produce. Then decide if it's worth the 30 minutes. (It is.)

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Skill identification and 60-day commitment initiation

**Tone note:** The parenthetical "(It is.)" at the end is a rare use of light humor that lands well in this otherwise direct brand voice. It assumes the reader will agree once they've done the math — which is almost always true. The two-year framing makes the value concrete and near enough to feel real.

---

## CTA 3 — Skills Development Program or Curriculum

**The line:**
If you want the structured path — which skills to build first, in what order, and how to develop them without wasting time on the wrong things — we built that map.

**Placement:** Mid-article, after establishing which skills actually compound

**Goal:** Skills curriculum, course, or program awareness

**Tone note:** "Which skills to build first, in what order" addresses the most common objection after the article convinces the reader of the concept: "I agree, but I don't know where to start." "Without wasting time on the wrong things" appeals to efficiency-oriented men who have already made mistakes with unfocused skill development.
