# Life Compass — Issue 5

**Subject:** What you're running from when you scroll

---

Nobody picks up their phone planning to lose four hours. Nobody sits down to game meaning to lose a Saturday. Nobody decides to drift — to let another month pass without moving toward anything.

It just happens. Which is exactly what makes it so hard to stop.

But something is driving the scroll and the game and the drift. And until you name it, you'll keep doing it.

Here's what's actually happening: you're running from the discomfort of building something that matters to you and might not work. That sounds simple. It isn't. Most men never articulate it that clearly.

Building a real life requires you to make decisions before you have certainty. To commit to things that might fail. To be honest with yourself about whether you're actually making progress or just making excuses. That's genuinely uncomfortable.

Your phone is right there, offering seventeen things that are easier.

Gaming deserves a fair look. Games get condemned often and usually for the wrong reasons. The real question is what gaming provides that's missing from a man's actual life.

Think about what a good game gives you: achievement, progress, clear objectives, visible evidence that you're getting better at something, and a community where your specific skills are needed and valued. The problem isn't the games. Those are real things a man needs. The problem is when games are the only place in his life that provides them.

Scrolling is different and, in some ways, worse. At least gaming requires you to do something. Scrolling is passive — consuming an infinite stream of other people's lives without contributing anything to the world. After a long scroll session you feel vaguely dissatisfied. Not rested, not better. You consumed a lot and produced nothing. That feeling is accurate.

Drifting is the hardest to see. You go to work. You handle the basics. Life looks fine from the outside. But you're not building. You're not moving toward anything. You're managing the present while the future recedes.

The tragedy of drifting is that it takes the same amount of time as building. The days pass either way.

The question isn't "how do I stop using my phone?" The question is: what are you avoiding, and what would you do instead if you had the courage to actually do it?

Answer that honestly and you've done more than most men will do this week.

**This week's anchor:** Spend the first 30 minutes after you wake up doing something that requires something from you — no phone, no content. Notice what comes up.

— Life Compass
