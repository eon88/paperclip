## Email Body

Here's the question most men can't answer quickly:

What will you not do, regardless of the incentive?

Not as an abstract philosophical exercise — as a practical test of whether you have a code. Because a code is not a list of vague values ("I value integrity"). It's a set of specific commitments that hold before the temptation arrives. The man who figures out his principles in the moment of pressure has no principles at all.

**Without a code, you're improvised.** Culture fills the gap. Peer pressure fills the gap. Convenience fills the gap. The man who hasn't decided what he stands for will stand for whatever is cheapest in the moment.

**With a code, you're anchored.** You know what you'll do and what you won't before the situation arises. That pre-commitment is what makes you trustworthy — to others, but far more importantly to yourself. And the man who trusts himself is free in a way that the undecided man never is.

A code doesn't need to be long or elaborate. But it needs to be:
- **Specific** — not "I value honesty" but "I tell the truth even when it costs me something"
- **Tested** — the code only becomes real when you've held it under pressure
- **Yours** — not inherited blindly, not performed for others, but genuinely arrived at and genuinely held

**This week:** Write the first draft of your code. Three to five statements, each one specific enough that you'd know immediately if you violated it. Don't make it aspirational — make it honest about what you actually hold yourself to right now and what you want to hold yourself to.

That draft is the beginning.

— The Digital Men Letter
