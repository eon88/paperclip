## Email Body

To you, at 18 —

You have more capacity than you know. You also have more wrong assumptions than you can currently see. Both things are true.

Here's what I wish someone had told me:

**On time:** You think you have all of it. You don't. Not in a morbid sense — but the habits you build in the next ten years will either compound into something real or calcify into a life that looks like everyone else's. The difference is not talent. It's what you do with the time.

**On women:** The best thing you can do for your romantic life is become genuinely worth knowing. Not performing it — being it. Do the work. Hold the standard. Tell the truth. The man who does those things doesn't need to chase.

**On discipline:** There is no shortcut. Everything worth having requires work that isn't always enjoyable. Build your morning. Learn to work when you don't feel like it. The feelings catch up eventually. Trust the system more than the mood.

**On brotherhood:** Pay attention to who you're spending your time with. You will become, to a significant degree, an average of the five men you're closest to. Find men who are going somewhere. Invest in them seriously. Be a real friend — honest, present, actually available.

**On faith and meaning:** Figure out what you actually believe. Don't defer the question. A man without fixed points gets pulled by whatever current is strongest that day. You need fixed points.

The honest summary: be less concerned with looking good and more concerned with becoming real. Real is rarer, harder, and worth more.

I'm proud of you already — not for what you've done, but for what you're going to choose to become.

— You, looking back
