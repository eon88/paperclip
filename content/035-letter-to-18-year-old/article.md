# A Letter to My 18-Year-Old Self

To you, at eighteen —

You have more capacity than you know. You also have more wrong assumptions than you can currently see. Both of those things are fine — the capacity is real, and the wrong assumptions are correctable. But you're going to spend a lot of energy on things that don't matter and not enough on things that do. So let me tell you what I wish someone had told me.

---

## On Time

You think you have all of it. You don't.

Not in a morbid sense — you have plenty of time. But you are currently spending it as if it's infinite, and the habits you build in the next ten years will either compound into something extraordinary or calcify into a life that looks like everyone else's. The difference between the man you can be at 30 and the man you'll probably be without this warning is not talent or luck. It is what you do with your twenties.

Read more than you currently do. Twice as much, minimum. Books — real ones, with arguments and ideas — are the fastest way to acquire 30 years of someone else's hard-won understanding. Most of the men you'll admire at 40 will have been readers since their twenties. Start now.

Take your body seriously. Not for aesthetics — for what it does to your mind, your discipline, and your presence. The man who has trained hard for five years carries himself differently than the man who hasn't. That difference compounds. Start the training you'll still be doing at 35. Start it now, before it feels urgent.

Waste less time performing. The social media posts, the persona management, the energy spent on how you look to people who don't matter — you're getting almost no return on this. The hours you spend building something real will produce something real. The hours you spend managing impressions will produce nothing. Redirect accordingly.

---

## On Women

You're going to spend more energy thinking about this than almost anything else. I'm not going to tell you not to — it's normal and it makes sense. But here's what I'd tell you:

The best thing you can do for your romantic life is become genuinely worth knowing. Not performing it — being it. Do the work. Build real skills. Hold real standards. Tell the truth even when it costs you. That combination is rarer than you think, and it will attract the kind of woman worth attracting.

Don't chase. The pursuing-without-self-respect version of you — the one who will compromise who he is to keep someone interested — will lose both the woman and himself. The version of you who knows what he's building and holds the standard will not need to chase.

You will be tempted to make someone the center of your life too early. Don't. Not because relationships don't matter — they matter enormously. But a man who doesn't have a mission of his own is not yet ready to be a good partner to anyone. Get the foundation right first. The relationship you want can only be built on something real.

---

## On Discipline

There is no shortcut here and I won't pretend there is.

Everything worth having requires work that isn't always enjoyable. The gap between who you are and who you could be is filled with hours of unglamorous, unrewarded, unwitnessed effort. The men who close that gap are not more talented than the men who don't. They are more consistent.

Build your morning. Whatever your day requires of you, start it with something that costs you something — training, reading, building. Not Instagram. Not reactive response to whatever the world throws at you first. Something you chose. Something that's yours. That one discipline, sustained for years, will compound into a man you'll recognize as real.

Learn to do the work when you don't feel like it. This is not about suppressing yourself — it's about understanding that feelings are weather, not commands. The man who only works when he's inspired will produce very little. The man who works consistently regardless of how he feels will produce an enormous amount over a decade. The feelings catch up eventually. Trust the system more than the mood.

---

## On Brotherhood

Pay attention to who you're spending your time with. This is more important than it feels right now.

You will become, to a significant degree, an average of the five men you are closest to. If those men are coasting, complaining, consuming — you will drift toward that. If they are building, holding standards, doing hard things — you will be pulled toward that instead. This is not about cutting people off harshly. It is about being intentional about who you invest in deeply.

Find men who are going somewhere. Not necessarily men who are already successful — men who are genuinely trying to become something. Train with them. Build with them. Have real conversations with them, not just performance.

And be willing to be a real friend — honest, present, actually available. The kind of friendship that tells a man when he's wrong, shows up when things are hard, holds the standard for each other without letting it slide. That kind of brotherhood is rare and it is worth building deliberately.

---

## On Faith and Meaning

You're going to have to answer some questions that feel easier to defer. What is this life for? What do I actually believe? What standard am I holding myself to and why?

Don't defer them. Not because I have the answers for you — I don't, and I shouldn't. But because a man who never engages these questions ends up living by someone else's answers by default, and often someone who doesn't have his best interests in mind.

Find out what you actually believe. Test it. Read the thinkers who challenge it. Wrestle with the hard parts. And then commit to something — a code, a conviction, a framework for what's worth doing and what isn't. Not because certainty is the goal, but because a man without any fixed points gets pulled by whatever current is strongest that day. Fixed points give you something to navigate by.

---

## The Honest Summary

You're going to waste some time. That's fine — it's inevitable and it's not the end. But the years from 18 to 28 are the ones you'll either build on or regret. Not because your life is over if you waste them — it isn't. But because the choices you make in those years determine the platform you're standing on when the serious years arrive.

Be less concerned with looking good and more concerned with becoming real. Real is rarer, harder, and worth more.

Do the work before you feel ready. You will never feel ready. The readiness comes from the doing.

Tell the truth more — to yourself first, then to everyone else. The lies you tell yourself are the most expensive ones.

Be patient with what's coming. The best things take longer than you want them to. But they come.

I'm proud of you already — not for what you've done, but for what you're going to choose to become. The fact that you're reading something like this means you already understand that choosing matters. That's more than most men your age understand. Run with it.

— You, looking back

---

**To every young man reading this:** You are not behind. You are not broken. You are at the beginning of the most formative decade of your life. What you do with it is not determined yet. It's still being written. Write it well.
