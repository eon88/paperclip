# CTA Options — Comfort Is Killing You Slowly

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
If this is landing, you need the newsletter. One piece a week. No noise. No motivation porn. Just the kind of thinking that makes you harder to fool — starting with yourself.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** The article ends with a challenge, not a handhold. This CTA mirrors that energy — it positions the newsletter as a continuation of an honest conversation, not a reward for reading. "Harder to fool — starting with yourself" is direct, slightly confrontational, and lands well with a reader who just been told he's been optimizing for the wrong thing. It respects his intelligence rather than flattering it.

---

## CTA 2 — Reflection Prompt

**The line:**
Before you move on: where has comfort been winning in your life — and what has that cost you?

**Placement:** End of article (below main text, above any links)

**Goal:** Reflection prompt / engagement

**Tone note:** The article does heavy diagnostic work. This CTA holds the reader in that space rather than rushing him to a next step. The phrase "where has comfort been winning" frames comfort as an opponent — which is exactly the reframe the article makes. No link. No ask. Just the question that does the most damage to the reader's comfortable self-narrative, which is the whole point. The best readers will sit with it. That's the goal.

---

## CTA 3 — Lead Magnet / Product Awareness

**The line:**
Knowing you've gone soft is the easy part. Building the daily structure that keeps you sharp — that's what this is for. Don't just read about it.

**Placement:** Mid-article — best placed after "You Don't Have a Discipline Problem. You Have a Safety Problem." section

**Goal:** Product/lead magnet awareness (e.g., daily discipline framework, hard habits guide)

**Tone note:** The article diagnoses the problem but the reader's immediate instinct is "so what do I do?" This CTA answers that question without over-promising. "Don't just read about it" is a mild provocation — the exact reader who keeps consuming content without acting is the one this is aimed at. The tone matches the article: no coddling, no hype, just a direct offer to someone ready to actually move.
