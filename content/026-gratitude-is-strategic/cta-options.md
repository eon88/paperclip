# CTA Options — Gratitude Is Not Soft — It's Strategic

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on operating at your best — not just working harder. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Operating at your best" vs. "working harder" mirrors the article's argument — this isn't about effort, it's about how you run. Speaks directly to the ambitious reader who already works hard but is looking for a performance edge.

---

## CTA 2 — 7-Day Gratitude Experiment

**The line:**
The experiment: 3 minutes every morning for 7 days. Before your phone, name three specific things that are real and working right now. Write them down. At the end of the week, compare your decision quality, your energy, and your mood baseline. The data will tell you everything.

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Behavioral experiment initiation

**Tone note:** Framed as an experiment rather than a prescription — "the data will tell you everything" positions the reader as the scientist, not the patient. Skeptical men respond to this framing. The "before your phone" instruction is specific and executable. The three metrics to track (decision quality, energy, mood baseline) give the experiment real measurement criteria.

---

## CTA 3 — Mental Performance Resource / Program

**The line:**
If you want a complete daily performance protocol — including the mental practices that keep the engine running clean for years, not just weeks — that's what this is built around.

**Placement:** After explaining what chronic dissatisfaction costs and what gratitude actually does

**Goal:** Program, course, or coaching product awareness

**Tone note:** "Keep the engine running clean for years, not just weeks" speaks to the long-game performance concern that the article identifies. Not promising happiness or spiritual transformation — promising sustained performance. That framing is precisely calibrated for the ambitious, skeptical reader.
