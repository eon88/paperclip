## Email Body

Most driven men dismiss gratitude.

They see it as contentment masquerading as virtue. As the thing people say when they've stopped wanting more. They've built their engine on dissatisfaction — on the gap between where they are and where they want to be — and they don't want to fill it prematurely.

This is a reasonable concern. And it's based on a false premise.

**Gratitude and ambition are not opposites.** They're not even in competition. Gratitude is oriented toward the present — toward what is real and working right now. Ambition is oriented toward the future — toward what hasn't been built yet. You can hold both fully and simultaneously.

What gratitude actually does — neurologically, not spiritually — is regulate cortisol. Lower baseline stress means better decisions. Better decisions mean more effective action toward the goals you're still hungry for. The man who is grateful AND hungry operates at a higher level than the man running entirely on dissatisfaction.

Because chronic dissatisfaction has a ceiling. It fuels the early stages. Then it burns through the reserve and produces the thing that destroys more serious men than failure: burnout.

**Grateful and hungry.** Not one or the other. Both.

**The practice (3 minutes every morning):** Before you open your phone, name three specific things that are real and working in your life right now. Not abstract — specific. Health. A relationship. A project you believe in. Let it land for a moment. Then plan your day.

That's it. Try it for seven days and watch what it does to your decision quality.
