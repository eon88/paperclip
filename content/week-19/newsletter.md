# Life Compass — Issue 19

**Subject:** Being needed is not the same as being liked.

---

Think about the last time someone genuinely needed you. Not wanted your company — actually needed you. The situation depended on you being there and coming through.

Think about how that felt. The focus that arrived without effort. The clarity. The willingness to do things you'd normally avoid.

Now think about how your average Tuesday feels.

The contrast tells you something real about your wiring.

Men have an instinct to matter in a concrete, immediate, someone-needs-me-right-now sense. This instinct is ancient. It was selected for across generations of survival — men who showed up when called, who provided and protected when things were at stake, passed on their wiring. You carry that.

But the modern world gives that instinct almost nothing to work with.

You live in an environment engineered to make you non-essential. Automation replaced functions that once required a man's skill and judgment. Entertainment absorbs your attention without asking anything of you. Your phone performs tasks that once required your presence and capability. The result is a generation of men who feel — at a biological level — redundant. Not useless to themselves. Unnecessary to anything outside themselves.

The research is consistent: men's wellbeing is tightly correlated with feeling needed. Not liked. Not affirmed. Needed.

Here's the thing you have to get clear on: validation and need are not the same thing. The current culture gives men validation in enormous quantities — likes, affirmations, performance reviews, constant "great work." Men mistake it for being needed because the social signal is similar. Someone is acknowledging you.

But the effects are completely different. Validation is about your ego. Being needed is about something outside yourself. Validation can be manufactured and inflated and ultimately means whatever the person giving it decides it means. Being needed is rooted in reality. Someone's wellbeing actually depends on you.

Men who live primarily on validation tend toward fragility — always needing more reassurance because the reassurance has no real roots. Men who are genuinely needed tend toward stability. They have something concrete to stand on.

If you find yourself constantly checking how you're perceived, needing external affirmation to feel okay, struggling to feel grounded — ask honestly: am I actually needed by anyone or anything right now? If the answer is no, the validation habit makes sense. You're trying to fill a real container with the wrong substance.

The fix isn't complicated: place yourself in contexts where your contribution genuinely matters. Commit to something that will fail if you don't show up. Take on a relationship where your attention makes a concrete difference. Build something that requires your sustained effort to exist.

**This week's anchor:** Identify one person or one commitment where you are genuinely needed — where your absence would cause a real cost. If you can't name one, that's your most important information right now.

— Life Compass
