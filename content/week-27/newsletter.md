# Life Compass — Issue 27

**Subject:** Do you have a clear picture of who you're becoming?

---

Most men have a vague sense of wanting to be better.

More disciplined. More focused. More worthy of the life they're living.

What most men don't have is a clear picture.

Not a vision board. Not a list of goals. A genuine, detailed, lived-in picture of the specific man they are trying to become — what he believes, how he moves through the world, what he has built, what he has refused.

This matters because direction without destination is just drift wearing a better costume.

The resistance to building a clear picture is almost always fear, though it rarely presents that way. "I don't want to box myself in." "I want to stay open." These are reasonable things to say. They are also, frequently, a way of avoiding the commitment that a clear picture requires. Because once you've built it, you've said your current self falls short. And that gap is demanding.

The man you're aiming at is not a fantasy. He is a projection — the version of you that has done the work, made the hard decisions, kept the commitments, and built what you are capable of building. He's not richer necessarily. Not famous. He is more solid. More directed. More at home in himself.

Build the picture with specificity. Not "I want to be disciplined" — what does a disciplined version of you do differently at 6am? Not "I want strong relationships" — specifically who are the people in your life, and what kind of presence are you in their lives?

A useful exercise: write a letter from the version of yourself ten years from now, back to you today. Not the letter you wish you could receive — the honest one. What did the next ten years build? What was hard? What was worth it? What did you have to give up?

This exercise does something abstract goal-setting doesn't. It puts you in the perspective of the man who has already done the work. From there, what actually matters tends to become clear.

Once you have the picture, use it. Every significant choice can be measured against it. Does this move me toward or away from who I'm trying to become?

Build the picture. Let it govern the choices that compound into who you become.

**This week's anchor:** Write a one-paragraph description of the specific man you are trying to become. Concrete enough that you could recognize him if you met him.

— Life Compass
