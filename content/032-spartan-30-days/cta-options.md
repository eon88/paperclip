# CTA Options — The Spartan 30-Day Reset

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up with Check-In

**The line:**
Subscribe to the letter and we'll check in with you at day 15 and day 30. You shouldn't do this alone.

**Placement:** End of article and newsletter

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up with engagement hook

**Tone note:** The check-in offer is the differentiator here — it converts the newsletter from a passive subscription to an active accountability touchpoint for the challenge. "You shouldn't do this alone" is direct and honest — the challenge is harder without structure and connection, and the letter provides both.

---

## CTA 2 — Challenge Commitment Prompt

**The line:**
Write down your start date. Write down the one habit from the list that will be hardest for you. Then tell one person — one real person — that you're doing this. That commitment makes it real.

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Behavioral commitment / social accountability initiation

**Tone note:** Three steps, in order, each one progressively harder to reverse. Writing the start date is private. Writing the hardest habit is self-aware. Telling one person is the commitment that makes the others stick. "That commitment makes it real" is honest — most solo commitments don't survive day 5.

---

## CTA 3 — Challenge Community / Accountability Group

**The line:**
If you want to do this with other men — a cohort that started on the same day and checks in weekly — that's what this community is built to facilitate. Come do it with us.

**Placement:** Mid-article or closing

**Goal:** Community / cohort membership for challenge participants

**Tone note:** The research on behavior change consistently shows that social context dramatically improves completion rates for difficult challenges. This CTA doesn't need to oversell that — "do it with us" is enough. The community offer is positioned as the difference between attempting the challenge and completing it.
