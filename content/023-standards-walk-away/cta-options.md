# CTA Options — The Man Who Knows When to Walk Away

---

## CTA 1 — Newsletter Sign-Up

**The line:**
One piece a week on building the kind of self-respect that makes the hard calls possible. Subscribe below.

**Placement:** End of article

**Goal:** Newsletter sign-up

**Tone note:** "Self-respect that makes the hard calls possible" speaks directly to the reader who just engaged with the article's central argument. It positions the newsletter as ongoing support for the exact work the article describes.

---

## CTA 2 — Standards Audit Prompt

**The line:**
Write down three answers: What am I currently tolerating in a friendship? In my work? In a relationship? For each one — write one sentence on why you're still there and whether that reason is real or just fear. That's the work.

**Placement:** End of article, closing challenge

**Goal:** Self-directed audit / reflection

**Tone note:** Specific to three life areas, which prevents the reader from only addressing the easy one. "Is that reason real or just fear" is the exact distinction the article makes — the CTA reinforces the key lesson while making it personal. No link needed.

---

## CTA 3 — Community Awareness

**The line:**
Holding standards is easier when you're around men who hold them too. That's one of the things the community is for — not accountability in a soft sense, but proximity to men who take these things seriously.

**Placement:** After the section on what holding standards actually looks like

**Goal:** Community membership / cohort awareness

**Tone note:** "Not accountability in a soft sense" distinguishes this from the typical accountability-partner framing that many men find unappealing. "Proximity to men who take these things seriously" is more honest about what the community actually provides — environmental influence toward higher standards.
