## Email Body

Let me ask you something direct.

How many times have you started the same goal? Same workout plan, same savings target, same creative project — and watched it fade out after a few weeks?

Most men blame themselves. Call it weakness. Tell themselves they just need more discipline, a better system, an earlier alarm.

That's the wrong diagnosis.

Men don't quit when things get hard. They quit when meaning runs out. When the goal stops feeling connected to anything real, the brain starts looking for an exit — and it finds one. Every time.

The fix isn't willpower. It's clarity.

**Before you chase harder, get clearer:**

- What is this goal actually *for*? Not why it sounds good. Why does it matter to *you*, specifically?
- What does winning look like in 90 days — concretely?
- What have you lost the last few times you quit this?

Most men skip this step. They copy goals from influencers, from social pressure, from vague ideas of who they should be — and then wonder why they can't sustain the effort. You can't run on borrowed purpose.

**Your reason has to be yours. Specific. Honest. Personal.**

"Get fit" dies in week two. "Stop being winded when I chase my kid around the yard" survives hard Mondays.

In this week's article, I break down exactly why men quit, why willpower systems fail without the right foundation, and what to do *before* you start — or restart — anything.

No fluff. No filler. Just the thing most productivity content skips entirely.
