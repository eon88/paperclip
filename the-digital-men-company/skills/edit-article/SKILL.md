## How to use this skill

Provide the full article draft from the write-article skill.

## What this skill produces

An edited article that is sharper, clearer, and stronger than the draft — without changing the voice.

## Editing process

Work through the draft in this exact order:

### Step 1 — Cut the fluff
Read every sentence and ask: does this give the reader something they did not have before? If the answer is no — if it restates, pads, or exists only to fill space — cut it. A 700-word article that delivers its full idea is better than a 1000-word article that repeats itself.

### Step 2 — Tighten the language
Look for:
- Passive constructions → make them active
- Filler phrases → cut them entirely
  - "it is important to note that" → just say it
  - "in order to" → "to"
  - "the fact that" → restructure the sentence
  - "kind of", "sort of", "in a way" → say it directly or cut it
- Weak qualifiers that undermine the voice → remove them

### Step 3 — Strengthen the opening
Read the first paragraph out loud. Ask: would a distracted man keep reading after this? If not, rewrite it. The opening must make the reader feel understood before asking anything of them. It must start in the middle of something true.

### Step 4 — Check the flow
Read the article from section to section. Does each section lead naturally to the next? If a transition feels forced or mechanical, either cut it or rewrite it so it earns its place. The reader should feel like they are moving forward, not being walked through a list.

### Step 5 — Sharpen the ending
The final paragraph should not summarize. It should conclude — land with weight and give the reader something to carry. Rewrite if needed.

### Step 6 — Mission alignment check
Ask these questions about the completed edit:
- Does this article treat the reader as capable?
- Does it avoid fake urgency, manipulation, or generic advice?
- Does the voice stay clear, direct, and grounded throughout?
- Is there any section that drifts toward preachiness or corporate language?
- Flag any issues for the CEO before handing off

## Output
The edited article with a one-line note on any significant cuts made and any mission alignment flags.
