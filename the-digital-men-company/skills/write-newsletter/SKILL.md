---
name: write-newsletter
description: >
  Adapt a completed article into a Life Compass weekly newsletter issue.
  Produces subject line, direct-address body (400–600 words), weekly anchor,
  and closing. One issue of 52.
---

## How to use this skill

Provide:
- The edited article
- Issue number (1–52)
- Phase number and name
- Any specific CTA or offer to include at the end (optional)

## What this skill produces

A complete Life Compass newsletter issue, structured as follows:

---

### Subject line
Short, direct, specific. Max 8 words. Describes exactly what this issue does for the reader. Not clever for its own sake. No question marks unless unavoidable. No "this week" or "issue #" in the subject line.

Examples of strong subject lines:
- "You were never taught what you were made for"
- "The feeling you can't shake has a name"
- "Comfort is the enemy you invited in"

### Opening line
One sentence. No warm-up. Start directly in the reader's experience. This line earns the next one.

### Body (400–600 words)
Adapted from the article — not copied. The newsletter is a letter to one person, not a broadcast to an audience. Key differences from the article:
- Shorter paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- More direct address — "you" throughout, not "men" or "young men"
- More personal in tone — this is one man writing to another
- Keep the core insight and the practical value; cut anything that doesn't translate to this format
- White space between paragraphs — email is scanned before it is read

### This week's anchor
One sentence. A single concrete thing the reader can think about or do this week based on what he just read. Phrased as a direct statement, not a suggestion.

Example: "This week, identify one area of your life where you have been drifting and write down what you actually want there."

### Closing
2-3 sentences, max. Brief, grounded, personal. No corporate sign-offs. No "have a great week." Write it as a man closing a letter to someone he respects. Optional: include the CTA if one was provided.

Sign-off: Life Compass

---

## Voice rules for the newsletter specifically
- More direct and personal than the article
- Write as if sending to one reader you respect
- No broadcast language ("hey everyone", "this week's issue")
- No fake enthusiasm
- The reader chose to receive this — respect his time and his intelligence
