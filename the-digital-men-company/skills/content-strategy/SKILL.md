## How to use this skill

Provide:
- **Topic** — the specific subject for this piece (e.g. "Why men feel restless even when life looks fine")
- **Phase** — the Life Compass phase number and name (e.g. Phase 1 — The Quiet Desperation)
- **Week number** — which of the 52 issues this is

## What this skill produces

### 1. Audience pain
Describe precisely where the reader is right now. Not "feeling lost" — describe the specific situation, the specific feeling, the specific moment this man is in when this topic is relevant to him. Make it so precise that a reader thinks "how did he know that?"

### 2. Core insight
The one true thing this piece will give the reader. The idea they did not have language for before reading it. One sentence.

### 3. Transformation
Where the reader starts and where they end. What changes in how they see their situation or themselves after reading this piece.

### 4. Title options (3)
Direct, strong, no clickbait. Titles that describe exactly what the piece does. No question marks unless genuinely unavoidable. No "how to" unless it is the most honest framing.

### 5. Outline (5–7 sections)
Each section listed with:
- Section title
- One sentence: what this section does for the reader

### 6. Opening hook
One paragraph. Names the reader's situation so precisely that he stops whatever he is doing and reads on. This is the most important paragraph in the piece. Get it right.

### 7. CTA direction
What is the natural next step for a man who just read this? What would serve him most right now? Keep it aligned with where he is in the Life Compass journey.

## Voice rules
- Clear, strong, direct, grounded
- Not corporate, not fluffy, not preachy
- Treat the reader as capable — he is not broken, the system that was supposed to form him failed
- Write like a man who has walked the road talking to a man who hasn't started yet
